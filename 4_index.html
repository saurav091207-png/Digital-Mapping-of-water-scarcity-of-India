Water Scarcity: Causes & Engineering Solutions

A comprehensive environmental breakdown compiled for EVS CA1[cite: 1].

Why is Water Scarcity Accelerating in India?

India supports 18% of the global population but possesses only 4% of the world's freshwater resources. The crisis is compounded by several structural and climatic factors:

  • Over-Extraction of Groundwater: Unregulated borewell drilling for intensive agriculture (especially paddy and wheat) has depleted confined rock aquifers.
  • Rapid Urbanization & Concretization: Unplanned metropolitan expansion replaces natural soil percolation beds with concrete, preventing rainwater infiltration.
  • Climate Change & Monsoonal Variability: Erratic rainfall patterns, shifting monsoon dates, and glacial retreat in the Himalayas reduce perennial river base flows.
  • Non-Revenue Water (NRW) Losses: Municipal distribution networks lose 35% to 45% of treated drinking water due to pipeline leaks.

4 Pillars of Urban Water Remediation & Solutions

To transition from impending "Day Zero" crises to hydrological self-sufficiency, cities must implement these 4 pillars:

1. Sponge Cities & SUDS Infrastructure

Replacing solid asphalt with permeable pavements, bioswales, and constructed wetlands that absorb 70-80% of storm runoff.

2. Managed Aquifer Recharge (MAR)

Directing rooftop rainwater and treated storm runoff into deep recharge shafts and defunct borewells.

3. Decentralized STPs & Dual Plumbing

Mandating on-site wastewater recycling plants in residential complexes (>50 units) for toilet flushing and gardening.

4. IoT Smart Water Metering & Acoustic Sensors

Deploying smart acoustic noise loggers across municipal pipelines to pinpoint underground leaks instantly.

Multi-Criteria Water Scarcity Index (WSI) Formula

To quantify urban vulnerability objectively, we utilize a weighted spatial decision index:

WSI = (0.30 × GWE) + (0.25 × PCD) + (0.20 × DFR) + (0.15 × TMR) + (0.10 × SWL)
  • GWE (30%): Groundwater Extraction Stage.
  • PCD (25%): Per Capita Deficit against CPHEEO 135 LPCD benchmark.
  • DFR (20%): Daily Volumetric Deficit Ratio.
  • TMR (15%): Private Water Tanker Reliance Percentage.
  • SWL (10%): Surface Water Encroachment & Lake Degradation.
